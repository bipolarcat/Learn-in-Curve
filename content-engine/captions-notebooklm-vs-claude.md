I pay for both NotebookLM and Claude Projects.

Not because I can't decide. Because they do different jobs, and I only worked out where the line sits after building quiz questions for 24 learning objectives.

Every project manager has the same pile. Contracts, specs, minutes, lessons-learned logs nobody reads. Both tools promise the same fix — point AI at your documents, get answers back.

The useful question isn't which one is smarter. It's which one you open on a Tuesday with 20 minutes to spare.

Here's what I found.

**Claude meters you.** Roughly 45 prompts per rolling five-hour window on Pro, shared across chat, Code and desktop. Anthropic doubled those limits on 6 May 2026 and it still bites mid-flow. The problem isn't the money — it's that you start rationing your iterations, and iteration is the whole point.

**NotebookLM charges you upfront, in effort.** You have to gather the sources before you get anything back. For a real research job, fair trade. For "I just want to understand this," it isn't — I end up on YouTube or talking to Gemini instead.

Then the bit that actually changed how I work.

Learn in Curve has 24 learning objectives. I needed quiz questions for each. The obvious move is one notebook with everything in it.

That's the wrong move.

NotebookLM's strength is that it stays inside its sources. So I gave it 24 separate notebooks, one per objective, each told plainly: this is the source of truth, do not go off topic. The questions came back on point every time. Cross-contamination between objectives never happened, because structurally it couldn't.

However — I didn't ship them on trust. Every question went back through Claude to check it made sense.

NotebookLM generated. Claude verified.

That's the whole relationship in one line. Deadline, go to Claude. Time to learn, go to NotebookLM.

One last thing nobody mentions: you're not choosing between two tools, you're choosing between two bundles. £18.99 gets you the notebook plus Gemini 3.1 Pro, Nano Banana, Antigravity and 5TB of storage. $17–20 gets you depth on one workflow.

Also worth knowing if you're in the UK — Google prices in pounds. Anthropic doesn't publish a UK price at all, so you're billed in dollars and the real cost moves with the exchange rate.

Honest caveat: I reach for Claude because I know how to drive it. Six to eight hours in NotebookLM against daily Claude use. Some of that preference is merit. Some is muscle memory. If you're starting fresh, that tilt doesn't apply to you.

What's your split between the two?

Two tools. Same promise: point AI at your documents, get answers back.

They are not interchangeable, and the difference matters most when you're short of time.

**NotebookLM** answers questions about your sources and refuses to wander outside them. That constraint is the feature. Give it 50 sources on the free tier, 300 on Google AI Pro at £18.99/month, and it will not invent context it hasn't been given.

**Claude Projects** works with your documents and hands something back — a draft, a decision, a document. 200k tokens of context, expanding roughly tenfold through retrieval. Claude Pro is $17/month billed annually, $20 monthly.

Where each one costs you:

→ Claude meters usage at roughly 45 prompts per rolling five-hour window on Pro, shared across chat, Code and desktop. You end up rationing iterations.

→ NotebookLM charges upfront in effort. Gathering sources takes longer than the answer is worth for casual learning.

Our founder Sim built quiz questions for all 24 APM PMQ learning objectives using both — 24 separate notebooks rather than one, so NotebookLM couldn't blur the objectives together, then Claude to verify every question made sense.

Generated by one. Verified by the other.

That's the pattern worth stealing.
