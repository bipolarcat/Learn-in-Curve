Learn In Curve

Brand Kit v1 — retro 70s, built for passing exams, not decoration.

01 — The Mark

One curve, recolored for the era it's borrowing from.

The existing S-curve mark (the "learning curve") stays — it's a good idea. What changes is the palette and warmth: black-on-white becomes ink-on-parchment, and the curve now runs between two mustard dots instead of a single flat point, like a groovy rainbow arc mid-swing.

Curve.

Minimum size: 28px icon-only, never below. Clearspace: half the mark's height on all sides. Never recolor the mark outside this palette — no gradients, no drop shadows beyond the standard "sticker" offset.

03 — Typography

Fraunces for character. Figtree for clarity.

Fraunces carries the groovy, swash-era warmth in headlines without tipping into novelty-font territory. Figtree keeps quiz questions and dashboard copy easy to scan at speed — this is a revision tool first.

Display / Fraunces 900 Pass the exam
Display italic / Fraunces 600i not the whole syllabus.
Body / Figtree 700 Which of the following best describes earned value?
Body / Figtree 500 Used across quiz options, dashboard labels, and tutor chat replies.
04 — Voice & Tone

A coach, not a lecturer.

Do

"You've nailed risk management — 3 sessions left on stakeholder engagement before your mock."

Don't

"Let's explore the fascinating world of project governance together! 🎉"

Direct, confident, specific to the user's actual weak areas. Never generic edtech cheerfulness — the product respects that the user has a demanding job and a deadline.
