1. What type of team environment characterises permanent organisational structures?
A) Temporary and dynamic
B) Stable teams working on routine or operational tasks
C) Teams brought together for specific initiatives
D) Teams that disband after project completion
Answer: B. Permanent structures, such as business as usual (BAU), have stable teams working on routine and operational tasks.

2. Which organisational structure involves individuals taking instruction solely from a functional line manager, resulting in straightforward communication?
A) Matrix
B) Project-based
C) Functional
D) Agile
Answer: C. In a functional structure, individuals take instructions only from a line manager, making communication simpler.

3. How is authority distributed in a matrix organisational structure?
A) Solely with the project manager
B) Balanced between the functional line manager and the project manager
C) Solely with the functional line manager
D) Controlled entirely by the project sponsor
Answer: B. A matrix structure allows the balance of authority between the functional line manager and the project manager.

4. Which structure forms core teams specifically for the entirety of a project and dissolves them after project completion?
A) Functional
B) Matrix
C) Project-based
D) Business as Usual (BAU)
Answer: C. A fully projectised structure brings teams together for a project and disbands them once complete.

5. What is a key disadvantage of a functional structure?
A) High administrative costs
B) Risk of silos and poor cross-department communication
C) Frequent team changes
D) Job security concerns
Answer: B. Disadvantages of a functional structure include the risk of silos, poor cross-department communication, and being less responsive to change.

6. Which temporary structure provides a mix of skilled resources on a temporary basis to a project?
A) Permanent
B) Functional
C) Matrix
D) Linear
Answer: C. A matrix structure provides a mix of skilled resources on a temporary basis to the project.

7. Which of the following is an advantage of a project-based structure?
A) Clear career paths within a specific skill function
B) Less responsive to change
C) High adaptability, focused decision-making, and strong team motivation
D) Specialised efficiency
Answer: C. Advantages of a project-based structure include high adaptability, focused decision-making, and strong team motivation.

8. In which structure are individuals most likely to be allocated repetitive tasks that match their specific capabilities?
A) Functional
B) Matrix
C) Iterative
D) Project
Answer: A. In a functional structure, allocated tasks match an individual’s capability and may be more repetitive and less varied.

9. Which structure combines functional and project-based approaches and features dual reporting lines?
A) Functional
B) Matrix
C) Project-centric
D) BAU
Answer: B. A matrix structure combines functional and project-based structures, functioning with dual reporting lines.

10. Which organisational structure is typically used for stable operations like manufacturing or standardised services?
A) Matrix
B) Project-based

Answer: C. A functional structure is best for stable operations like manufacturing or standardised services.

B) Iterative or hybrid

24. How do delegated responsibilities increase efficiency during the project lifecycle?

Answer: B. A Responsibility Assignment Matrix (RAM or RACI) can support defining roles and providing stakeholder oversight.

26. Which project life cycle provides maximum control and governance due to its sequential structure and clearly defined outputs?
A) Iterative
B) Hybrid
C) Linear
D) Agile
Answer: C. A linear lifecycle is sequential with defined outputs, providing maximum control and governance.

27. Which life cycle requires greater flexibility in governance procedures because the required solution or scope may initially be unclear?
A) Linear
B) Iterative
C) Sequential
D) Permanent
Answer: B. An iterative lifecycle allows objectives to evolve and requires greater flexibility in governance procedures due to vague scope.

28. What does a hybrid life cycle combine?
A) Functional and Matrix team structures
B) Linear and Iterative approaches
C) Project Sponsors and Product Owners
D) BAU and Operations
Answer: B. A hybrid lifecycle combines both Linear and Iterative lifecycles to enable a mixed approach.

29. Why is governance embedded into each phase of the project life cycle via control points like decision gates?
A) To dissolve the project team periodically
B) To ensure the requirements of a preceding phase are met before work progresses to the next phase
C) To immediately escalate all budget changes
D) To switch from a linear to an iterative life cycle
Answer: B. Governance ensures requirements of a preceding phase are met before progressing, relying on assurance at decision gates.

30. In a linear lifecycle, what is typically required if a change to the strict budget or schedule is needed?
A) Approval from the product owner
B) Adaptation by the team before the next iteration
C) Escalation to the steering board for approval
D) Changing the organisational structure
Answer: C. A linear approach typically requires any change in budget or schedule to be escalated to the steering board for approval.

C) By hiring more permanent BAU staff
