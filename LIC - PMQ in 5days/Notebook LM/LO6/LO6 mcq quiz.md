**1. In a linear life cycle, when are decision gates held?**
A) Time-bound during iterations
B) At the end of each stage
C) During benefits realisation
D) During project handover
*Correct Answer: B. In a linear life cycle, decision gates are event-driven and held at the end of each stage.*

**2. How are decision gates structured in an iterative life cycle?**
A) Event-driven
B) Time-bound
C) Ad-hoc
D) Unscheduled
*Correct Answer: B. In an iterative life cycle, decision gates are time-bound and held at the end of each iteration.*

**3. Who primarily needs to provide agreement at a decision gate?**
A) The independent auditor
B) The project sponsor and governance board
C) The end-user
D) The supply chain contractor
*Correct Answer: B. Decision gates primarily aim to gain agreement from the sponsor and wider governance board.*

**4. What do decision gates check to ensure the project is on track?**
A) That the earned value exceeds the planned spend
B) That the project will deliver the benefits defined in the business case
C) That team motivation is high
D) That the audit findings have zero non-conformances
*Correct Answer: B. Decision gates check that the project is still on track to deliver the benefits defined in the business case.*

**5. When are benefits reviews typically held?**
