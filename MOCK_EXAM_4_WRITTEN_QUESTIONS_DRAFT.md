## Long-form (5 marks each, 50 marks total)

### L1 — LO6 (Reviews)

**Question:** You are project manager on a residential construction project. A recent site inspection found recurring plastering defects across twelve units. The site team has been mentioning "some issues" verbally in weekly calls for a month, but nothing has been formally reported, and the sponsor only learned the scale of the problem by chance during a site visit.

Explain (a) two categories of information that should have been produced and shared to inform decision-making earlier, and (b) one reason the project's plans may now need to be re-planned as a result.

**Model answer:** (a) Two categories: status reporting — clear reporting of progress and quality position against the plan's acceptance criteria, so the sponsor understands the cost, schedule and quality position rather than relying on informal comments in calls; and risk identification — formal identification and escalation of the emerging pattern of defects as a risk (or, once confirmed across twelve units, an issue), so corrective action or escalation happens while there is still time to act, rather than the sponsor discovering the scale by chance. (b) One reason: the planned scope is not achieving the required quality, so remediation work for twelve units needs to be added to the schedule and budget, and the baselined plan updated to reflect the additional rework and any knock-on delay to handover.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark per information category correctly identified, up to 2. 1 mark per category correctly explained with a reason it should have informed earlier decision-making, up to 2. 1 mark for a valid re-planning reason linked to the scenario. Cap 5.

### L2 — LO7 (Assurance)

**Question:** You are project manager on a government digital service replacing a paper-based benefits application process. The programme's internal audit function has proposed a very heavy assurance schedule — weekly deep-dive reviews of every workstream, regardless of risk level — and delivery leads are pushing back, arguing it will slow the programme without adding value.

Explain three of the criteria an effective assurance plan should meet, and use them to explain what is wrong with the audit function's current proposal.

**Model answer:** An effective assurance plan should be: (1) Independent, objective and proportionate to the work — proportionate meaning the level of scrutiny should match the level of risk, not be applied at maximum intensity everywhere; the current proposal applies the same heavy weekly review to every workstream regardless of risk, which is disproportionate and will slow low-risk work for no benefit. (2) Targeted at the greatest risks — coverage should follow the risk register rather than being applied as a flat schedule; the proposal does not appear to differentiate by risk level at all. (3) Coordinated in its timing and cycles — reviews should land before key decision gates so findings can inform decisions, rather than running to a fixed weekly cadence disconnected from the programme's actual decision points.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark per assurance-plan criterion correctly named, up to 3. 1 mark per criterion correctly applied to explain the flaw in the audit proposal, up to 3. Cap 5.

### L3 — LO11 (Conflict Resolution)

**Question:** You are project manager on a new product design team. Two designers strongly disagree about the direction of a key feature — one wants a simpler, faster-to-build version, the other believes it under-delivers on user needs and is willing to argue the point repeatedly in team meetings. The disagreement has slowed the last two design reviews, but it has also surfaced a usability risk neither designer had raised before.

Explain (a) one positive impact this conflict has already had on the project, and (b) two negative impacts the project risks if the conflict is not addressed.

**Model answer:** (a) One positive impact: the disagreement has surfaced a new usability risk that neither designer had previously raised — a direct benefit of open debate, since conflict can bring hidden risks to the surface that would otherwise stay hidden until later, when they would be more expensive to fix. (b) Two negative impacts: continued disagreement is diverting management time away from other design decisions as reviews keep stalling on this one issue; and if left unresolved, it risks lowering team morale and damaging the working relationship between the two designers, making them less willing to raise concerns openly with each other in future.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark for a valid positive impact of conflict correctly linked to the scenario. 1 mark for explaining why that positive impact matters (surfacing risk early). 1 mark per valid negative impact correctly linked to the scenario, up to 2. 1 mark for explaining why at least one negative impact matters, beyond simply naming it. Cap 5.

### L4 — LO15 (Ethics, Compliance and Professionalism)

**Question:** You are project manager on a cloud-migration project for an asset-management firm, moving client portfolio data to a new hosting provider. During due diligence, you discover the hosting provider's ISO 27001 information-security certificate appears to have been altered — the certification body's logo and the accreditation number don't match. The provider is a long-standing partner, and your sponsor is keen not to damage the relationship over "what might just be a formatting issue."

Explain (a) why you have a professional duty to act rather than let this pass, and (b) two sources of specialist advice you would consult before deciding how to proceed.

**Model answer:** (a) Professionals have a duty to "speak up" and report wrongdoing through an organisational process — this is treated as a professional obligation, not an optional courtesy, especially where the discrepancy touches information security for client financial data. Letting a potentially altered security certificate pass unchallenged because of relationship pressure from the sponsor would itself be a compliance and ethical failure, regardless of whether it later turns out to be innocent, and it needs formal escalation rather than an informal conversation. (b) Two sources: APM's Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct, for guidance on personal conduct and the duty to raise concerns; and the organisation's legal team or external lawyers, for advice on the compliance and contractual implications of a potentially falsified security certificate before any further client data is migrated.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark for correctly naming the professional duty to "speak up" or report wrongdoing. 1 mark for a valid explanation of why relationship pressure does not override that duty in this context. 1 mark for identifying that this needs formal escalation rather than an informal conversation. 1 mark per valid, distinct source of specialist advice correctly named, up to 2. Cap 5.

### L5 — LO17 (Solutions Development)

**Question:** You are project manager evaluating options for new imaging equipment for a hospital trust. Options include buying the equipment outright, leasing it, or entering a managed-service partnership with the manufacturer. The trust also needs to decide whether to roll the new equipment out to all six sites at once or phase it in gradually.

Explain (a) two criteria types you would use to evaluate the buy/lease/partnership options, with an example of what each considers, and (b) which transition approach — phased or "big bang" — you would recommend and why.

**Model answer:** (a) Two criteria types: Procurement — weighing lease vs buy vs make vs service vs partnership as live options, considering upfront cost against ongoing commitment and flexibility to upgrade equipment as imaging technology changes; and Management — considering the life cycle and governance implications of each option, since a managed-service partnership requires a different, ongoing governance relationship with the manufacturer than a simple purchase. (Technical — future-proofing against imaging technology changes — or Social criteria could also be credited if justified.) (b) A phased rollout across the six sites, because it lets lessons learned and any issues with the new equipment or workflow be identified and corrected at the first site or two before the trust commits to a full six-site rollout, reducing the risk of replicating a problem across every site simultaneously, which a "big bang" approach would risk.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark per criterion type correctly named, up to 2. 1 mark per criterion correctly paired with a scenario-relevant example, up to 2. 1 mark for a valid transition-approach recommendation with justification. Cap 5.

### L6 — LO18 (Quality Management)

**Question:** You are project manager on a customer-service transformation for a telecoms provider. The business case promises a 10-point improvement in Net Promoter Score (NPS) within twelve months of go-live. The programme board has asked how the quality plan will demonstrate this promise is actually being delivered, not just assumed.

Explain (a) why quality indicators need to be explicitly linked to the business case in this project, and (b) two elements the quality plan should define so the NPS promise can be properly tracked.

**Model answer:** (a) Quality indicators tie back to the business case so the project can prove it has delivered the value it promised. Without a measurable indicator tied to the NPS commitment, the programme board has no way of confirming whether the transformation actually improved customer experience, or whether any change in NPS is coincidental. Quality planning answers "what does good look like?", and here "good" is explicitly defined by the business case's 10-point NPS target. (b) Two elements: methods of verification — how NPS will actually be measured, such as a defined post-interaction survey methodology applied consistently before and after go-live; and frequency — how often NPS will be measured and reported, such as monthly, so trend data, not a single snapshot, demonstrates progress towards the twelve-month target.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark for a valid explanation of why quality indicators must link to the business case. 1 mark for correctly linking this to the specific NPS promise in the scenario. 1 mark per quality plan element correctly identified and applied to NPS tracking, up to 3. Cap 5.

### L7 — LO19 (Integrated Planning)

**Question:** You are project manager brought in six months into a £9m rail-depot upgrade that started without a single integrated project management plan — each function (engineering, safety, commercial) has been working from its own separate documents. Two disputes have already arisen between engineering and commercial about what was actually agreed on scope, and a new safety inspector joining next month will need to get up to speed quickly.

Explain (a) two problems this project has experienced as a direct result of not having an integrated PMP, and (b) two reasons producing one now would help, referencing the specific situations described.

**Model answer:** (a) Two problems: different team members have been working to different assumptions, since engineering, safety and commercial each hold separate documents rather than one source of truth — this is the direct cause of the disputes between engineering and commercial about what was agreed on scope; and there has been no baseline against which to measure progress or manage change, meaning scope disagreements cannot be resolved by referring back to an agreed position. (b) Two reasons: it would act as a single source of truth, so future disagreements between functions can be resolved by referring to one agreed document rather than each function's separate version; and it would serve as induction material, letting the new safety inspector get up to speed from one integrated document covering scope, resources and management strategies, rather than being talked through three separate, potentially inconsistent sets of documentation.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark per problem correctly identified as resulting from the lack of an integrated PMP, up to 2. 1 mark per problem correctly linked to the specific dispute or onboarding situation in the scenario, up to 2. 1 mark for a valid additional reason producing the PMP now would help. Cap 5.

### L8 — LO21 (Resource Management)

**Question:** You are project manager on a mobile app development project. Your organisation has a fixed team of six developers who cannot be increased — no budget for contractors and no other developers available internally. The current schedule shows three critical features all requiring development in the same two-week period.

Explain (a) which resource optimisation technique is appropriate given the fixed team size, and how it differs from the alternative, and (b) two practical actions you could take to apply it to this two-week clash.

**Model answer:** (a) Resource levelling is appropriate, because the resource pool of six developers is fixed and cannot flex, so the schedule must be adjusted to fit the available resource rather than the other way round — levelling answers "with the resources available, when will the work be finished?" This differs from resource smoothing, which keeps the end date fixed and instead adjusts resource demand within available float (for example, adding people or parallelising tasks); smoothing is not viable here because there is no ability to add developers, and the schedule end date may need to move as a result of levelling. (b) Two actions: redefine or extend the duration of one or more of the three features so they no longer all require full development effort in the same two-week window, sequencing them where dependencies allow; and reprioritise which of the three features is genuinely critical, using requirements priority such as Must-haves, so the team's fixed capacity is applied to the highest-value feature first and lower-priority features shift later in the schedule.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark for correctly naming resource levelling with a valid reason linked to the fixed team size. 1 mark for correctly distinguishing levelling from smoothing. 1 mark for correctly noting the schedule end date may need to move as a result. 1 mark per practical levelling action correctly described, up to 2. Cap 5.

### L9 — LO4 (Business Case)

**Question:** You are project manager on a £5m factory-automation business case for a manufacturer. Since approval three months ago, a major competitor has launched a cheaper automated product line, and a new energy-efficiency grant has become available that could offset some of the project's cost.

Explain (a) three of the five elements of a business case, describing what each would need to reflect given this new information, and (b) why the business case should be reviewed now rather than left until the next scheduled decision gate.

**Model answer:** (a) Strategic context — needs to reflect the shifted competitive landscape now that a cheaper rival product exists, and whether the automation project still delivers the intended competitive advantage. Economic analysis — the return-on-investment appraisal should be revisited, since the new energy-efficiency grant changes the cost side of the appraisal and could improve the case's value for money. Financial case — affordability and funding source should be updated to reflect potential grant income, which may free up budget or shorten payback. (b) The business case is a living document that should be reviewed whenever significant change occurs, not only at scheduled gates. Both the competitor launch and the new grant are material changes that affect whether the benefits and costs originally approved are still accurate, and waiting until the next scheduled gate risks the project continuing on assumptions that are no longer valid — the grant might reduce cost now, or the competitive threat might require a scope change sooner than the next gate allows.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark per business case element correctly named, up to 3. 1 mark per element correctly applied to the competitor or grant information, up to 3. 1 mark for a valid reason to review now rather than waiting for the next gate. Cap 5.

### L10 — LO12 (Leadership)

**Question:** You are project manager on a highway resurfacing programme. A recently qualified graduate engineer seems increasingly disengaged — missing minor deadlines and contributing less in meetings — despite being paid fairly and having good working conditions. Separately, an experienced site foreman who has delivered strong results for fifteen years has started pushing back on being told exactly how to sequence tasks, saying he "just needs the outcome, not step-by-step instructions."

Explain (a) which motivation model best explains why good pay and conditions alone have not kept the graduate engineer engaged, and what it suggests you do next, and (b) how you would adapt your leadership style differently for the graduate engineer versus the site foreman, and why.

**Model answer:** (a) Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory: pay and working conditions are hygiene factors — they prevent dissatisfaction but do not by themselves create motivation. The engineer's disengagement suggests the missing ingredients are motivators, such as recognition, achievement, responsibility or growth; the next step is to identify which motivator is missing (for example, a lack of recognition for work done, or no clear development path) and address it directly, rather than assuming pay and conditions are sufficient. (b) For the graduate engineer: a more coaching-oriented, supportive style, giving personalised guidance and feedback to rebuild engagement and help them develop capability and confidence, since disengagement in a new starter often follows unmet motivator needs rather than unwillingness to work. For the site foreman: a more delegative style, giving him the outcome and stepping back on the how, since he is an established, high-performing team member who — per McGregor's Theory Y — is self-motivated and takes pride in ownership; continuing to direct his tasks closely would demotivate rather than support him.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark for correctly naming Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory. 1 mark for correctly distinguishing hygiene factors from motivators and applying this to the engineer. 1 mark for a valid coaching-style recommendation for the graduate engineer with reason. 1 mark for a valid delegative-style recommendation for the site foreman with reason. 1 mark for correctly referencing Theory Y (or an equivalent correct justification) for the foreman. Cap 5.

## Short-form (2 marks each, 14 marks total)

### S1 — LO5 (Procurement)

**Question:** In the context of contract negotiation, state what BATNA and ZOPA each stand for, and briefly define one of them.

**Model answer:** BATNA = Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement; ZOPA = Zone of Possible Agreement. BATNA is the most advantageous fallback course of action a party can take if the negotiation fails. ZOPA is the range within which an agreement is possible, based on the overlap between the buyer's maximum and the seller's minimum.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark for correctly expanding both acronyms. 1 mark for a correct definition of at least one of them. Cap 2.

### S2 — LO2 (Governance Arrangements)

**Question:** State two project governance roles, other than the Project Manager and Project Sponsor, and one responsibility of each.

**Model answer:** Any two of: Project Steering Board/Group — provides strategic direction, authorises the business case, sanctions funding, decides on escalated issues. Product Owner — defines product scope, prioritises the backlog, and accepts delivered increments (in iterative or hybrid life cycles). Users — define requirements and acceptance criteria. Project Team — performs tasks and delivers outputs, reporting progress.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark per correctly named role with a valid responsibility, up to 2. Cap 2.

### S3 — LO9 (Benefits Management)

**Question:** State the difference between an "end benefit" and an "intermediate benefit," giving one example of each.

**Model answer:** An end benefit aligns directly with a strategic objective and is often only confirmed months after project closure, for example a sustainable office that supports long-term growth. An intermediate benefit is realised during the project and supports the eventual achievement of the end benefit, useful for demonstrating progress before the end benefit can be confirmed, for example reduced energy bills or improved staff engagement along the way.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark for a correct distinction between end and intermediate benefit. 1 mark for a valid example of each. Cap 2.

### S4 — LO14 (Diversity and Inclusion)

**Question:** State two reasonable adjustments a project manager could make to help team members with different needs contribute fully.

**Model answer:** Any two of: providing assistive technology or software for a team member with a visual or hearing impairment; adjusting meeting format or providing materials in advance for a neurodivergent team member; offering a quiet workspace or flexible break patterns for someone managing a health condition; translating key materials or providing an interpreter for a team member whose first language differs from the working language.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark per valid, distinct reasonable adjustment, up to 2. Cap 2.

### S5 — LO24 (Change Control)

**Question:** State two things that could go wrong if project plans are not updated after a change has been approved.

**Model answer:** Any two of: the team works from outdated information, wasting time and money; conflict and disengagement follow as people work to different versions; delivered outputs may end up redundant; stakeholders lose confidence in the project; other projects or business-as-usual work are adversely affected; the business case's benefits may not be realised.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark per valid, distinct consequence, up to 2. Cap 2.

### S6 — LO13 (Team Management)

**Question:** State two characteristics of an effective project team.

**Model answer:** Any two of: clear roles and responsibilities aligned to skills and experience; a shared sense of accountability; open and honest communication; mutual trust and respect; a common sense of purpose or shared objectives; appreciation of the diverse strengths each member brings.

**Marking guide:** 1 mark per valid, distinct characteristic of an effective team, up to 2. Cap 2.

### S7 — LO16 (Requirements Management)

**Question:** State the four steps of the requirements management process, in order.

**Model answer:** Gather (identify stakeholders and capture requirements); Analysis (use a technique such as Function Cost Analysis to understand value, resolve conflicts, and feed back to stakeholders); Justifying requirements (prioritise, e.g. using MoSCoW, and report priorities back to stakeholders); Baseline needs (set the agreed requirements baseline for managing scope and change).

**Marking guide:** 1 mark for naming at least two of the four steps correctly. 1 mark for naming all four steps correctly, roughly in order. Cap 2.
