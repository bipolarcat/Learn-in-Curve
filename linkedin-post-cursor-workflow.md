I'm a project manager with zero software engineering background. Three months ago I didn't know what localhost was.

I built the first version anyway. Plain HTML, Claude as my teacher, one accordion of learning objectives split across five days. It took ten days and about half of that was spent waiting for usage limits to reset.

Version two took three weeks. Next.js, Supabase, auth, payments, an AI tutor called Sly. The difference wasn't that I'd got better at coding. I still can't write a line of it.

The difference was Cursor, and how I put it to work.

⏱️ **A month to spend, not a five-hour window**
Cursor gives you a monthly budget. So I built for seven days straight, then stopped for a week. On version one I lost days to rolling limits, and the waiting is what doubled my timeline, not the building.

🔌 **Two agents, one shared file**
Cursor wrote the code. Claude reviewed it against the codebase and found real mistakes. However, neither knew what the other had done, and I was pasting summaries between them like a middleman. So I made both write to the same business-state.md file after every change. That one file is what turned three weeks of repeating myself into a week where I couldn't stop building.

🎯 **Time-box the pretty bits**
I've got an architecture degree and I will happily lose three hours to a footer. So every time I caught myself refining, I asked the question I'd ask on site: is this on the critical path, and what does it actually buy me?

Version one got 192 sign-ups. Version two has one paying user.

Turns out building it was the easy part. Getting it in front of people is the actual project.
