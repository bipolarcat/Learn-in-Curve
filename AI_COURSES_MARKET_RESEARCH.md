# AI Courses for Learn in Curve — Market Research & Shortlist

**Prepared:** 18 August 2026
**Scope agreed with Sim:** audience = any project manager regardless of certifying body; credential approach and format left open for the research to recommend; deliverable = written report with a shortlist.
**Method:** four parallel research streams (supply, credentials, demand, whitespace), ~120 web fetches total. Every load-bearing figure in the "Bottom line" was then re-verified by me against its primary source. Claims that could not be primary-sourced are labelled as such and should not go into marketing copy.

---

## 1. Bottom line

**The obvious course is the wrong one.** Your instinct — "PMs are scared of being left behind by AI, sell them AI skills" — points directly at the single most crowded, most commoditised, fastest-decaying corner of this market: *using AI tools to do ordinary PM work*. Coursera/IBM, LinkedIn Learning, Udemy, Scrum Alliance and a £599 Knowledge Train workshop all already sell it, much of it is free elsewhere, and prompt engineering as a paid topic is visibly in decline. If you build there you compete on video production against companies with more of it than you.

**The defensible course is the one nobody has built: how to actually deliver an AI project without it failing.** RAND (Aug 2024, 65 practitioner interviews) puts AI project failure at **over 80%, twice the rate of non-AI IT projects** — and the number one cause is not technical, it's *leadership failing to communicate what problem they want solved*. That is a project management failure, described in project management language, and it is currently being taught almost exclusively to data scientists. No PM-facing course operationalises it.

**And there is exactly one forcing function in this whole landscape.** EU AI Act **Article 4 (AI literacy) has been legally binding since 2 February 2025** — verified against Article 113's application schedule, not a blog summary. It binds *deployers*, not just AI companies. That is the only thing in this market that makes an organisation legally obliged to train its people. The UK has no equivalent as of today.

**Recommended sequence:**

| | What | Why | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Flagship (paid)** | *Delivering AI Projects* — managing AI/ML as the deliverable | Genuinely unserved for PMs; RAND gives you an unarguable hook; suits your quiz/scenario engine | B2C, your existing funnel |
| **2. Funnel (free)** | *AI for Project Managers* — tools, prompting, safe use | Commoditised as a product, but excellent as a lead magnet and brand asset | B2C, top of funnel |
| **3. Verify then decide** | *CPMAI in 5 Days* — prep for PMI's AI certification | Perfect fit for your engine, proven model — **but blocked on one licensing question** (§9) | B2C, identical to PMQ |
| **4. Hold, deliberately** | *AI Literacy for Delivery Professionals (Article 4-aligned)* | The only offer with a legal forcing function and real budget behind it — but it's a B2B sale you aren't set up for yet | B2B, later |

**But read §2 before you act on any of this.** You already wrote the decision rule that governs this question, and it says "not yet."

## 2. Your own decision rule already applies here

`PMP_COURSE_DECISION.md` (Aug 2026) answers a structurally identical question — *should we build a second course?* — and answers no, for reasons that transfer directly:

> "Your advantage in PMQ came from a weak competitive field… You would be trading a market you can win for a bigger one you probably cannot, before you have proven you can win anything."

And it set five conditions, all of which must be true before a second course gets built: 250+ organic sessions/week to the Library, free mock converting at 30%+ to email, PMQ signups compounding from a named channel, real demand impressions in Search Console, and verified external exam stability.

**Those conditions are not AI-specific. They are about whether your machine works at all.** None of them have changed because the topic changed from PMP to AI. Applying your own rule honestly: **do not build an AI course now.**

**Where the AI question genuinely differs from the PMP question — and this is the part worth your attention.** The decisive argument against PMP was the strong competitive field. That argument splits in two here:

- **The "AI tools for PMs" course fails the same test PMP failed.** Crowded field, big incumbents, commoditising content. It is the PMP mistake in a different costume — and it happens to be the course your instinct pointed at.
- **"Delivering AI projects" passes it.** Field is genuinely weak: PMI and APMG gesture at governance, ILX sells a £2,775 corporate framework, and nobody has operationalised data readiness, model drift or non-deterministic acceptance criteria for a PM audience. **A weak competitive field in a real market is precisely the condition that made PMQ winnable.**

So the AI opportunity is real, and it is specifically *not* the obvious one. That is a finding worth holding onto — but it doesn't override the sequencing.

**The cheap move that fits your existing playbook.** PMP_COURSE_DECISION's answer to "I want a second product sooner" was: write Library pages, generate demand evidence at near-zero cost, let Search Console tell you if the demand is in *your* audience. Do exactly that here. Two or three Library pages — *"Why AI projects fail (and what project managers get wrong)"*, *"AI in project management: what's actually changed"*, *"Do project managers need an AI certification?"* — cost you days, not months. They test the demand, they feed the acquisition channel you're already trying to prove, and the AI angle is more link-worthy than PMQ content, so it may pull harder in search than anything else in the Library.

If those pages pull, you have evidence. If they don't, you saved a quarter of content work.

**One further caution.** Your activation funnel says the biggest leak is orient→learn, 39% lost. Adding a fourth SKU to a funnel that loses four in ten users at step two multiplies the leak rather than fixing it.

Being explicit about this, because the difference matters when you put numbers on a sales page.

**Verified directly against primary source:**

- **EU AI Act Article 4 applies from 2 February 2025.** Confirmed via Article 113: Chapters I and II apply from 2 Feb 2025; remaining provisions 2 Aug 2026; Article 6(1) 2 Aug 2027.
- **RAND: >80% AI project failure, 2x non-AI IT.** Published 13 Aug 2024, 65 semi-structured interviews (50 industry, 15 academia). Root causes in order: leadership/communication (84%), data quality (60%), technology-chasing (32%), infrastructure, immature tech.
- **APM/Censuswide survey, n=1,000, published 9 Sept 2025:** 70% of organisations using AI (up from 36% in 2023), 29% planning, 1% neither. Top challenge among AI users: **technical knowledge/training, 49%**. Then security/privacy 44%, workflow integration 42%, inaccuracy/untrustworthiness 41%. Construction adoption went 15% → 75% in two years.
- **PMI-CPMAI exists and is live.** Full name "PMI Certified Professional in Managing AI (PMI-CPMAI)™". 120 questions, 160 minutes, proctored. **No experience prerequisite.** Renewal: 30 PDUs every 3 years. Bundle includes a 21-hour prep course.

**Could NOT verify — do not treat as fact:**

- **CPMAI exam fee.** The widely-quoted $699 member / $899 non-member comes from third-party training resellers. **PMI's own certification page does not state a fee.** Before you build anything on this, get the number from PMI directly.
- **Whether third-party CPMAI prep is permitted.** PMI bundles its own 21-hour prep course. Whether an independent provider may prepare candidates and say so is unresolved and is the go/no-go for option 3.
- **"Gartner: AI will eliminate 80% of PM tasks by 2030."** This is a **2019 claim and the primary source URL now 404s.** It is a zombie stat still being recycled as current. **Do not use it in marketing** — an unsubstantiated claim like this is precisely what the ASA rules against (§7).
- **Individual PM willingness to pay for AI training.** No PM-specific data found. The nearest figure is general-US-workforce (47.8% spent an average $788 on self-funded upskilling), which is a different population.
- **UK salary data for "AI project manager" titles.** US figures only ($102,682 average, ZipRecruiter 2025).
- **Real practitioner sentiment.** Reddit/PM community access failed within budget. "PMs are anxious" remains an assumption, not a finding.

## 4. The supply side: what's actually being sold

The market splits into three products that buyers conflate. Getting this distinction right is most of the strategy.

**(a) "Use AI tools to do PM work"** — the crowded tier.
Coursera/IBM *Generative AI for Project Managers* (published June 2024, no visible refresh — over two years stale in a field that moves quarterly). LinkedIn Learning has at least four overlapping similarly-named courses, which tells you no one there owns the topic. Udemy's leading dedicated course has **664 students and 16 ratings** — thin, which cuts both ways: the term isn't saturated, but it also isn't proven to sell. Scrum Alliance sells the cheapest structured version (~$75, 4 hours, microcredential). Knowledge Train sells a UK one-day workshop at **£599 with a certificate of attendance only**.

**(b) "Manage AI/ML projects as the deliverable"** — the credentialed tier.
PMI-CPMAI. APMG's *AI-Driven Project Manager*. ILX's *AI Project Governance Framework* at **£1,000–£2,775**. All priced for corporate L&D budgets.

**(c) "General AI literacy"** — not PM-specific.
Google AI Essentials, Section, Reforge. Note that Maven/Reforge "AI PM" courses mean **product** manager, not project manager. Different buyer. That mislabelling is an opportunity, not competition.

**Three exploitable weaknesses:**

1. **APM has no AI course and no AI credential.** A blog post, an interest network, and a directory of other people's courses. For a UK, APM-adjacent audience that is a wide-open authority gap.
2. **There is nothing credentialed between £599 (workshop, no credential) and £2,000+ (corporate framework cert).** A self-funding individual PM has no mid-tier option.
3. **The market leaders' content is old.** A 2024 GenAI course in 2026 is not a small problem — it predates the entire agentic tooling shift.

## 5. What the demand evidence really says

**Employer adoption is real and verified. Individual willingness to pay is not.**

The APM data is strong: 70% adoption, near-doubling in two years, and — the number that matters most for you — **49% of AI-using organisations name technical knowledge and training as their top challenge**. That is a training gap stated by the buyer, in a UK sample of 1,000, published by the body your audience already trusts.

But the money is moving in an awkward direction. A July 2026 US survey (n=600 HR leaders) found employers cutting training budgets by an average $126k while increasing AI tool spend by $88k, with 53.7% willing to trade slower skill development for AI investment. If that holds, PMs will increasingly have to fund their own AI training — which is a reasonable case for a consumer product, but it is *inferred*, not evidenced.

**The framing implication is important, and it cuts against your original premise.** The evidence supports selling *competence* ("you'll run AI projects that don't fail"), not *fear* ("AI is coming for your job"). The fear framing rests almost entirely on a 2019 Gartner claim that can no longer be verified at source. Fear-based marketing here is both weakly evidenced and legally exposed.

**Which specific skills, ranked by evidence:**

1. Applied AI inside existing PM work — scheduling, resource allocation, risk forecasting, reporting (each ~50%, APM)
2. Data and performance analysis with AI (28%/26%)
3. Knowing when *not* to trust AI output — inferred from the 41% citing inaccuracy and 44% citing security/privacy as barriers. Under-taught and genuinely valuable.
4. Admin automation (25%)

## 6. The strategic frame: where your engine is the moat

This is the part I'd push hardest on as a co-founder.

**Learn in Curve's asset is not content. It's the assessment engine** — structured learning objectives, 2,000+ questions, scenario MCQs, mock exams, spaced review, and Sly grounded in the syllabus. You built that for exam prep and it is genuinely good at exam prep.

That engine is a moat **only where there is something to be assessed against.** Where the product is "watch me use ChatGPT", your engine is irrelevant and you're competing on video against Coursera. Where the product is "here is a body of knowledge, and here is proof you've mastered it", your engine is the whole product and almost nobody else has one.

So the filter for every candidate course is: **can this be examined?** That single test reorders the shortlist and it is why I'm steering you away from the tools course as a paid product and toward AI project delivery and Article 4 literacy — both of which are assessable, and one of which (Article 4) has a compliance buyer who specifically *wants* an assessment record as evidence.

## 7. The shortlist

### Option A — *Delivering AI Projects* — **flagship, recommended**

For PMs who are being handed AI projects and finding their normal playbook doesn't work.

Covers: why AI projects fail differently (RAND's five causes); data readiness assessment before you commit a delivery date; writing acceptance criteria for a non-deterministic system; model drift and what "done" means when the thing degrades after launch; evaluation instead of testing; why rigid agile fits AI badly (RAND says this explicitly — a strong hook for a PRINCE2/PMQ-trained audience); shadow AI and vendor due diligence; the business case and why 95% of GenAI pilots show no measurable ROI.

- **Unserved:** yes. CPMAI and APMG touch governance; nobody operationalises data readiness, drift, or non-deterministic acceptance criteria for a PM audience.
- **Engine fit:** excellent. This is scenario-MCQ territory — exactly what you already generate well.
- **Credential:** your own certificate of completion (legally fine — see §7).
- **Risk:** no external exam, so the certificate carries less weight. Mitigate with substance, not claims.

### Option B — *AI for Project Managers* — **free lead magnet, not a paid product**

The tools course. Prompting, AI in Asana/Jira/Copilot, safe use, what not to trust.

- **Unserved:** no, heavily served.
- **Why do it anyway:** it is what people search for. It is the top of the funnel into Option A, it is genuinely useful, and per your own 45/55 framing it earns brand and learning value even if it converts nothing. Do not charge for it.
- **Warning:** highest content-decay rate of anything here. Budget for quarterly refresh or don't ship it.

### Option C — *CPMAI in 5 Days* — **verify first, then it may be the best option of all**

Prep for PMI's Certified Professional in Managing AI. Structurally identical to PMQ in 5 Days: real external exam, real fee, motivated candidates, your engine doing exactly what it was built for.

- **Engine fit:** perfect. Nothing else here matches it.
- **Blocker:** two unknowns — the actual PMI exam fee (not published on their page), and whether independent providers may offer CPMAI prep and say so. PMI operates an Authorised Training Partner programme; if prep is ATP-gated this option dies or becomes a partnership conversation.
- **Action:** one email to PMI. Cheap to resolve, and it's the highest-information question in this report.

### Option D — *AI Literacy for Delivery Professionals (EU AI Act Article 4)* — **the real commercial prize, wrong motion for now**

Article 4 requires providers *and deployers* of AI systems to ensure staff have "a sufficient level of AI literacy". Binding since 2 Feb 2025. Any organisation deploying AI systems in scope has an obligation it is very likely not currently evidencing — and an assessment record is exactly what a compliance buyer wants to hold.

- **Unserved:** the generic "AI literacy for all staff" market exists; **nothing is framed for PMs, PMOs and delivery leads**, who are the people actually procuring and deploying these tools.
- **Why hold it:** this is a B2B compliance sale — procurement, long cycles, a buyer who is not your current audience. You are a solo founder with a consumer funnel. Right prize, wrong moment.
- **When to revisit:** once Option A exists, it is 70% of the content you'd need, and you could sell it to employers as a bundle.

### Option E — *Change Management for AI Adoption* — **whitespace, but weak fit**

Genuinely underserved for a PM audience, and arguably the biggest real-world problem. But it's soft-skills content — hard to examine, which means your engine adds little and you're back to competing on video. **Fold the best of it into Option A as a module rather than building it standalone.**

## 8. Legal flags

*Informal, educational guidance — not legal representation. The items marked ★ need real solicitor review before anything goes live.*

**Your own certificate is fine. Your claims about it are the risk.** Nothing in UK law stops an unaccredited provider issuing a "certificate of completion". The exposure is entirely in what you *imply*. Under the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 — now enforced with direct civil powers by the CMA under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 — it is a misleading action to present information suggesting a course is accredited or confers a professional-body qualification when it does not. CMA guidance to education providers is explicit that accreditation status must be disclosed accurately **at the research stage, before purchase**, not after signup.

Safe: "certificate of completion", "certificate of achievement", clearly scoped to what the learner did.
Not safe: "qualification" used in a way implying regulated status, implied Ofqual recognition, or any professional-body endorsement you don't hold and can't evidence.

**★ The relevant ASA precedent** (Course Accreditation Ltd, 2025) turned on unsubstantiated recognition claims and implied partnerships — logos displayed without a real arrangement, "globally recognised" with nothing behind it. The lesson is narrow and useful: never display a body's logo or claim recognition without a documented relationship.

**★ Retire the Gartner stat.** "AI will eliminate 80% of PM tasks by 2030" is 2019, unverifiable at source, and using it as a current fact in advertising is an unsubstantiated claim — the exact failure mode the ASA rules on.

**★ If you ever sell Option D, be extremely careful not to imply the course discharges an Article 4 obligation.** You can say it *supports* compliance. Saying it *makes them compliant* is both wrong and the highest-risk claim in this entire report.

**★ Option C requires clearing PMI's trademark and prep rules** before you say "CPMAI" anywhere near a sales page.

**Bonus finding for the existing product:** APM's PMQ syllabus has no AI content, so there is no compliance reason to update PMQ in 5 Days. But the **APM Body of Knowledge 8th edition is in consultation and AI was raised**. Worth a watch item — BoK and PMQ syllabus have historically moved on different cycles, so BoK publication is the signal, not the trigger.
